Why it exists

Most planners are websites wearing a calendar.

Planner Pro is a small application you own. It loads before you finish deciding to open it, and it still works when the network doesn't.

Fast by construction

No framework, no CDN, no web fonts. One CSS file, eleven small scripts, and a single render pass per view. There is nothing to hydrate.

Yours to keep

One JSON document in your browser, exported with a click. No account, no sync service, no terms of service standing between you and your own week.

Fits the screen you have

Three columns on a desktop, an icon rail on a tablet, a bottom bar on a phone. Tested down to 320 px wide with no sideways scrolling.

What's inside

Small surface, no dead ends.

Every screen does one job. Nothing is a placeholder.

Today, at a glance

Completion ring, streak, next time block and open-task count — plus the one line that says what would make today count.

Time blocks that know the time

The block you are inside right now is highlighted, so the day view reads like a schedule instead of a list.

Real weeks

A weekly plan belongs to that week. Navigate back and it is still there; next week starts clean instead of repeating last week forever.

Year, month by month

Twelve month cards with goals and real completion progress, plus a year theme and the goals that outlive a Tuesday.

Command palette

Ctrl/⌘ + K searches every task, note, block and goal you have ever written, runs commands, and jumps to dates — try next friday.

Carry-over, not guilt

Unfinished tasks move to today in one tap, and every delete has an undo — with a 30-day trash behind it. Nothing is lost by accident.

Archive instead of delete

Finished years move aside automatically. Restore one, export it, or remove it — the planner stays light without losing history.

Four themes, done properly

Midnight, Ocean, Light and System — each with its own contrast-correct palette, not an inverted filter.

No networking code

Not “privacy friendly” — absent. There is no fetch, no XHR, no socket in the source. The only way anything leaves the device is a file you choose to export.

Your notes, as plain files

Export the whole planner as a vault of Markdown notes — one per day, one per week — ready to open in Obsidian or any text editor. No lock-in, no conversion.

A week that closes properly

A review on the day you choose: what got done, what didn't, what changes — and one thing that becomes next week's focus. Days close the same way.

Backups you can trust

One click exports AES-256 encrypted, passphrase-locked JSON — safe to drop in Drive or Dropbox. Your key never leaves the browser.

See the year, not just today

A heatmap of every day, your best streak, the weekday you actually finish things — and the tags you use most. Click a square to open that day.

Routines, not repeats

Daily, weekdays or the days you choose. Save a whole day as a template — blocks and tasks — and apply it to any date in two taps.

Honest about time

Put minutes on a task and the day shows planned time against your capacity, turning amber when you have booked more hours than exist.

Type like a person

Quick add understands “call the clinic tomorrow 4pm #health” — date, time and tag, filled in before you finish the sentence.

Profiles with a PIN

Separate planners on one device, each behind a 4-digit PIN that is asked for on launch. No email, no recovery, nothing to leak.

Privacy

Your week is not our data.

There is no backend to send anything to. The app makes no network requests once the shell is cached — check the Network tab with the connection off.

  • Local storage onlyOne versioned document in your browser, readable and exportable.
  • No analyticsNo scripts from third parties, no beacons, no crash reporting.
  • No accountProfiles are local. A PIN is a lock, not a login — nothing to recover, nothing to leak.
  • Export anytimeJSON for backup and restore, Markdown to keep your notes forever.

What leaves the device

Nothing. The only outbound links are the ones on this page and in the app's About screen.

Clearing site data deletes your planner, so export a backup from Settings → Backup now and then. The app reminds you.
